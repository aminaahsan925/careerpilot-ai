# CareerPilot UI/UX — 3 FRESH directions, round 2 (synthesis, 2026-11-20)

Synthesised from round 2 research on Pinterest + Dribbble (16 best references),
run after Amina rejected Direction 2 (Dark Analytics) on 20 Nov 2026 because
it looked like AI-generated generic work and did not grab her attention.
Round 2 brief: designs that stop the scroll in the first second; NO generic
SaaS dashboards. Sources:
- Dribbble: UI-EXPLORATION-R2-DRIBBBLE-2026-11-20.md (10 shots, 8 best)
- Pinterest: UI-EXPLORATION-R2-PINTEREST-2026-11-20.md (12 pins, 8 best)

The two sources converge on the same three directions. Both researchers
independently recommended mixing them by feature (brutalism for the landing,
mascot for the recruiter, magazine for jobs/roadmap) — offered as a fourth pick.

## Fresh Direction 1 — Poster Brutalism ("type is the hero")

Feel: giant condensed type, black/white/red/acid yellow, numbered manifesto
rows instead of cards. Confrontational copy. The furthest thing from the
rejected dashboard.

Palette: white, black, signal red, acid yellow; monochrome variant (black /
white / grey only) or khaki + dark red.
Type: extra-bold condensed grotesque, all caps, tight leading; heavy black
sans, stacked lines.

What it changes in CareerPilot:
- Landing hero: "GET HIRED. NO RULES." / "TURN YOUR DEGREE INTO A JOB." at
  poster scale, or "RESUMES THAT GET READ." over a red circle and a real
  student portrait.
- Features as numbered poster rows: 01 Resume, 02 Roadmap, 03 Market Mirror,
  04 AI Recruiter — no cards.
- Confrontational stance: "Your resume is being ignored." / "Stop applying.
  Start being chosen." — the AI recruiter as the challenger.
- "The Drop": weekly job drops and limited-time recruiter screens, urgency and
  scarcity, plus a student-story magazine; CareerPilot as a season, not a
  portal.

References:
- Pinterest: Brutalist Web Design 2026 (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1050464681855130157/ — AI modified),
  Bold Brutalist black/white/red (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/996280748848372397/ — AI modified),
  Brutalist dashboard skin (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1150951248551126147/ — AI modified),
  "TURNING AMBITION INTO ACTION" (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/486951778483340390/ — no AI label)
- Dribbble: SELF-ESTEEM COURSE (https://dribbble.com/shots/27779456-SELF-ESTEEM-COURSE-Bold-landing-page-concept),
  IRON THEORY (https://dribbble.com/shots/26791566-Brutalism-Ecommerce-Website-Design-IRON-THEORY)

Honest risk: the loudest direction. It will grab attention, but it must stay
usable — brutalism done badly feels hostile, and the in-product screens need
the same discipline as the hero.

## Fresh Direction 2 — Editorial Magazine ("CareerPilot as a magazine")

Feel: a magazine cover, not a dashboard. One saturated colour field, one huge
word, photography + serif/script contrast, masthead + cover lines + sections.

Palette: vermilion red #E50700 / red-orange #D41607, newsprint cream, black;
peach-cream #FAE3D6 + lime #A7B216 variant; burnt gold + black variant.
Type: oversized display type as the hero (serif masthead or condensed sans),
thin sans for rules, handwritten script signatures as contrast.

What it changes in CareerPilot:
- Landing hero: one huge word ("HIRED.") on a saturated field, with a 3D
  object (paper plane, cap, growing plant) rising; or a student walking through
  giant "CAREER" letters.
- Masthead "CAREERPILOT" with cover lines for each feature; "Latest Jobs" and
  "Editor's Roadmap" as magazine sections; pull-quote from a hired student.
- Job Market Mirror as a gallery: "The Hiring Season" exhibition, employers as
  artists, roles as exhibits, Apply as a ticket; resume analysis as a portrait
  critique.
- Roadmap as growth/bloom chapters (FLOURISH) or manifesto chapters
  ("We build hires, not resumes." — MONTGOMERY).
- Make the student the star: "Meet [Name], hire-ready" — portrait + huge name
  + 3 proof points (Drake).

References:

  Museum Landing Page (https://dribbble.com/shots/27246494-Editorial-Web-Design-Museum-Landing-Page),
  Drake (https://dribbble.com/shots/26739229-Drake-Personal-Portfolio-Website-Bold-Landing-Page-UI-Design),
  MONTGOMERY (https://dribbble.com/shots/27613722-MONTGOMERY-Webflow-view),
  Festival Landing Page (https://dribbble.com/shots/27421012-Festival-Landing-Page-UI-Editorial-Web-Design)

  GAZU (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1129418412827895083/ — AI modified)

Honest risk: the safest strong bet for the landing page, but a magazine look
needs real photography and real student stories to work — stock filler would
put it straight back into generic territory.

## Fresh Direction 3 — Character-Led / Living World ("the AI has a face")

Feel: playful, illustration-led, interactive. A mascot with a face, a living
scene, a dashboard organised as places instead of metrics. Toy-bright colours,
tilted collage instead of aligned cards.

Palette: mustard yellow, orange-red, green, lime green, navy, periwinkle,
chocolate brown + cream + sky blue; teal water + koi orange/gold variant.
Type: chunky rounded bold sans; bold sans headlines with large numerals.

What it changes in CareerPilot:
- The AI Recruiter gets a face: a googly-eyed "Pilot" mascot (Plopp) that
  reacts to the resume score, asks "Ready for your first interview?" and pops
  job matches; or a 3D pilot/guide that breaks out of the screen (3D bunny).
- The learning roadmap as a friendly loop / streak with a mascot and
  encouraging copy (Loop); the resume score as a face that changes expression.
- The Job Market Mirror as a living pond: real jobs as koi that dart toward /
  away from the cursor, dropping a resume in creates a ripple, matched jobs
  swim to the student (Koi Pond — needs original illustration/motion).
- Home screen as a map of destinations: industries / cities as illustrated
  places to explore, with weather-style signals for hiring demand (Santorini).
- The student's personality as the product: illustrated student characters, a
  tilted collage of resume/roadmap/job cards, "your personality is the
  hireable part" (Bold Personality Illustration).

References:
- Pinterest: Plopp (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1110348483167540910/ — no AI label, most transferable mascot idea),
  Loop (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/599823244171784644/ — no AI label, real Figma case study),
  Koi Pond (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1090293391090353373/ — AI modified),
  Santorini Explorer (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1137158974708283865/ — AI modified)
- Dribbble: 3D Creation App UI (https://dribbble.com/shots/26794473-3D-Creation-App-UI-Playful-Web-App-Design),
  Bold Personality Illustration (https://dribbble.com/shots/22849731-Bold-Personality-Illustration)

Honest risk: the friendliest direction, but the mascot must be original — a
generic AI-made blob would repeat the exact rejection. It also needs care to
stay credible for serious hiring decisions, not childlike.

## The mix (what both researchers recommended)

Poster Brutalism for the landing page (furthest from the rejected dashboard),
the Plopp/Loop mascot as the AI Recruiter's face inside it, and magazine
sections for jobs and roadmaps — so the three CareerPilot features each get a
different visual hook instead of one dashboard.

## AI-label warning (Pinterest round 2)

5 of the 8 best Pinterest pins are labelled "AI modified" (Brutalist Web
Design 2026, Bold Brutalist black/white/red, GAZU, Koi Pond, Santorini).
Treat them as mood / composition references only — rebuild with real
photography, original illustration and a real mascot. The three non-labelled
pins (READER, Plopp, Loop) are the closest to real designer work. No
AI-labelled shots were reported in the Dribbble round.
