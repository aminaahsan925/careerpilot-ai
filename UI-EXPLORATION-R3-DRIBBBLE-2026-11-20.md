# UI Exploration R3 — Dribbble: Motion-First Modern AI Product Websites
## For CareerPilot (AI career product for university students: AI resume analysis, learning roadmap, job market mirror, AI recruiter)

- Date: 2026-11-20
- Source: Dribbble (dribbble.com), searched and opened live on 2026-11-20
- Searches run: `ai-website-landing-page`, `cursor-parallax-website`, `interactive-hero-animation`, `ai-saas-landing-motion`, `modern-ai-dashboard-animation`, plus targeted follow-ups `liquid-glass-cursor-effect-hero`, `3d-ai-sphere-interactive-hero`, `3d-interface-ai-product`
- Shots opened: 11 shot pages. Every link below was opened and title-checked on its own shot page. Two IDs taken from search-result listings resolved to the wrong shots (one was a travel platform, one a logo) and were dropped/corrected, not trusted blindly.
- Quality bar applied to every pick (per the 2026-11-20 update): the design must be SCREENSHOT-WORTHY — modern, aesthetic, impressive enough that someone would screenshot it and say "wow, this is so good". It must NOT look generic and must NOT look AI-generated. A clean but ordinary layout does not pass. Each shot below carries a one-line "Why it passes the screenshot test".
- Motion bar: motion is the point. A beautiful static shot that does not move is not what was wanted, so 7 of the 8 are video/animated shots or describe their interaction. The one static exception (#5 Lumora) is flagged as such and is included only for its visual concept, with the motion to add spelled out.
- Excluded on purpose: mascot/cartoon characters, kids-app looks, generic SaaS dashboard screenshots.

## The 8 best shots

### 1. Liquid Glass Cursor Effect Hero for WordPress — Slider Revolution
- Link: https://dribbble.com/shots/27876130-Liquid-Glass-Cursor-Effect-Hero-for-WordPress
- Format: video shot, 4.6k views
- What it shows: Four fullscreen slides, each with its own identity. Giant lime panel reading "SHIFT YOUR VIEW"; a monochrome portrait; a spa slide ("Quiet Space") with a glowing glass lamp; a dark fashion slide. Minimal top nav.
- Palette (Dribbble palette): #040403 (near-black), #E1F10D (neon lime), #5F5E5D, #9D9C9C, #D1D1D0, #ADB528, #565917, #817C61
- Type style: Oversized black grotesque capitals for "SHIFT YOUR VIEW"; an elegant serif for the quiet/spa slide; minimal small nav type.
- Motion — EXACTLY: A pointer-driven liquid-glass lens follows the cursor and stretches into an organic, reflective shape as it moves; it bends, magnifies and refracts the typography and imagery beneath it in real time. Monochrome portraits reveal colour underneath the lens. The cursor illuminates a glow inside the glass lamp. Each slide has its own look, and the lens is the unifying interaction.
- Why it passes the screenshot test: A glass lens refracting giant lime type, with a monochrome portrait turning to colour underneath it — a cursor toy, not a layout.
- CareerPilot takeaway: The lens is the perfect metaphor for resume analysis — drag the lens over a resume and it magnifies the score, the missing skills, the hiring-bar verdict.

### 2. 3D AI Sphere Interactive Hero Animation — Augustus
- Link: https://dribbble.com/shots/26887694-3D-AI-Sphere-interactive-hero-animation

- CareerPilot takeaway: Make CareerPilot itself an orb. Type a goal ("I want a data analyst job") and the orb listens, processes, then answers. The orb's state changes per module: scanning (resume), pathing (roadmap), pulsing (job market), screening (recruiter).

## 3 design directions distilled from the 8 shots

### Direction A — "The Living Orb" (a cursor-reactive AI persona)
- Distilled from: Shot 2 (3D AI Sphere), Shot 8 (Zajno 3D Interface), Shot 6 (Abstract Hero ribbons).
- Palette: Near-black #060606 / #03010C; deep violet #271599 / #180D5A; iridescent pink-purple #8F5381 / #9851D4; lilac #D7C6F3; hot-red accent #F7381E used sparingly.
- Type: Clean modern sans; huge thin/light grotesque headline; minimal copy — the sphere is the visual, the type gets out of its way.
- Motion pattern to copy: One central interactive 3D orb as the CareerPilot persona. It tilts/rotates with the cursor, reacts to typing with "listening" / "processing" states, and sits in a slow diagonal aurora that also drifts with the pointer. The hero is otherwise minimal; an "Ask anything" bar sits under the orb. The orb's state changes per module: scanning (Resume Analysis), pathing (Learning Roadmap), pulsing (Job Market Mirror), screening (AI Recruiter).
- Best for: The hero. The most ownable direction — two independent studios (Augustus, Zajno) landing on the same sphere-as-persona idea is a strong signal.
- Risk: A sphere alone can feel empty; it needs the ask-anything bar and the state changes to be a product, not a screensaver.

### Direction B — "Cursor as Flashlight / Lens" (the pointer reveals the insight)
- Distilled from: Shot 1 (Liquid Glass), Shot 7 (Shadow & Light), Shot 3 (Kiln spotlight borders).
- Palette: Near-black #040403 / #010101; neon lime #E1F10D / #9BF80D; warm stone #C6C5BF; ember #FF7A45 as the alternate accent.
- Type: Oversized grotesque capitals for the statement ("SHIFT YOUR VIEW" / "LESS GUESSWORK"); an elegant serif for the reveal moments ("Follow the light", "Quiet Space").
- Motion pattern to copy: The cursor is a tool, not a pointer. A glass lens magnifies and refracts what sits under it; a flashlight lights hidden detail; spotlight borders follow the cursor across bento cards; kinetic text morphs on scroll. The lime accent survives a light/dark toggle.
- CareerPilot mapping (the reason this direction fits the product): Lens/flashlight over a resume reveals the AI score and what is missing; over the Job Market Mirror it reveals which skills are in demand; over an AI Recruiter verdict it reveals why a candidate passed or failed a real hiring bar. "Reveal" is what the product does — the motion should do it too.
- Best for: The product's core interaction and the section transitions. Recommended as the lead direction if one has to be chosen: it is the most distinctive and the most tied to what CareerPilot actually sells.
- Risk: Flashlight/lens effects can hurt readability if overused; keep them to hero + key reveals, and keep body text untouched.

### Direction C — "Warm Dark Bento in Motion" (the motion-first product page)
- Distilled from: Shot 3 (Kiln), Shot 4 (OmniAgent), Shot 5 (Lumora, visual only).
- Palette: Warm dark #020201; warm off-white #EBE6DF; a single ember accent #FF7A45 (or lime #9BF80D); Lumora's thermal orange-purple #FBA168 / #43218D reserved for the talent-constellation moments.
- Type: Cabinet Grotesk (display) + Satoshi (UI/body) + JetBrains Mono (scores and metrics) — Kiln's exact named stack, as the starting point.
- Motion pattern to copy: A bento grid (4 → 2 → 1 columns) where every card proves itself on hover — cursor-tracked spotlight borders, subtle 3D tilt, magnetic CTAs, blur-in scroll reveals with no layout shift, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. Floating cards frame the hero (OmniAgent: student outcomes, recruiter reactions, job cards, not a product screenshot). The Job Market Mirror becomes Lumora's talent constellation, rebuilt with cursor parallax on the starfield and orbiting skill nodes.
- CareerPilot mapping: One bento card per pillar — Resume Analysis, Learning Roadmap, Job Market Mirror, AI Recruiter — each with its own hover behaviour and its own metric (resume score, roadmap progress, market demand, recruiter verdict).
- Best for: The full product page below the hero. The most buildable direction — Kiln was built as a real working site in HTML/CSS/vanilla JS, so its motion spec is proven, not just rendered.
- Risk: Bento grids are the most common AI-landing pattern in the set; it only passes the screenshot bar if the single accent, the warm dark, and the hover motion are all kept disciplined.

## Recommendation
- Lead with Direction B (Flashlight/Lens) for the hero and reveals, build the page on Direction C (Warm Dark Bento) below it, and use Direction A (Living Orb) as the CareerPilot persona inside the hero — the orb is what the flashlight reveals. Do not ship a flat gradient background, a static dashboard screenshot, or a mascot.
- Next step: prototype the hero with (1) a cursor lens/flashlight over a sample resume score, (2) the orb with listening/processing states, and (3) one Kiln-style bento card with spotlight + tilt, then judge all three against the screenshot test before building the rest.
