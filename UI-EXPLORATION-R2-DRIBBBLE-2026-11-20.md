# CareerPilot UI/UX exploration, round 2 — Dribbble findings (2026-11-20)

Fresh search after Amina rejected Direction 2 (Dark Analytics) as looking like
AI-generated generic work that does not grab attention. Brief: designs that
stop the scroll in the first second; NO generic SaaS dashboards (dark-with-glow
and light-indigo-cards explicitly excluded). No sign-in used. All 8 searches
ran (bold landing page, editorial web design, creative SaaS landing, playful
app UI, brutalist website, illustration dashboard, typography website design,
unique career platform); 10 shots opened. The creative-SaaS and
illustration-dashboard searches confirmed the rejection: their top results were
mostly black/white KPI dashboards and generic SaaS heroes, so none were
selected. One Dribbble human check appeared and was cleared (image-grid
challenge on the SELF-ESTEEM shot).
Source task: browser-task:8483cabd-1851-578a-8f0d-7379ef563964 (ran 17:19-17:24 PKT).

## 3 distinct visual directions

A. **Editorial Poster / Magazine** (FLOURISH, Museum, Drake, MONTGOMERY). One
   saturated colour field, one huge word, photography + script/serif contrast.
   Safest strong bet for CareerPilot's landing page.
B. **Provocative Brutalist / Drop Culture** (SELF-ESTEEM COURSE, IRON THEORY).
   Confrontational copy, monochrome or khaki/red, numbered manifesto, limited
   weekly "job drops" and a magazine. Highest attention, highest risk — best
   for a campaign / hero stance.
C. **Playful Character-Led** (3D Creation App UI, Bold Personality
   Illustration). A mascot/illustrated student breaks the frame, toy-bright
   mustard/orange/green, tilted collage instead of aligned cards. Best for the
   in-product experience and for making the AI recruiter feel friendly to
   students, not corporate.

## Recommendation from the evidence

Lead with Direction A for the landing page (FLOURISH/Museum scale +
MONTGOMERY manifesto), borrow Direction B's drop/magazine mechanic for the Job
Market Mirror, and use Direction C's mascot for the AI recruiter inside the
product — so the three CareerPilot features each get a different visual hook
instead of one dashboard.
